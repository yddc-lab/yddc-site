As AI technology rapidly develops, people are increasingly turning to AI not only to answer questions or complete tasks, but also to have personal or meaningful conversations. Funded by a Fordham Faculty Research Grant, the CHAT Study examines individuals' experiences and attitudes toward using AI chatbots for conversational purposes, aiming to understand who is most likely to seek AI support and how these interactions relate to mental health and interpersonal relationships. The study also explores how past experiences and values shape these choices, and how human-AI conversation quality varies by topic and across individuals.

As part of this study, participants complete a baseline survey assessing their mental health, relationship quality, and attitudes toward AI, and take part in an experimenter-facilitated Zoom session with three short conversations with a lab-managed ChatGPT account. In addition, participants complete a brief survey after each conversation and a short follow-up survey two weeks later to capture how their experiences and attitudes change over time.The CHAT Study is among the first to examine how people experience AI chatbot conversations. The study findings may inform understanding of the conditions under which AI engagement is supportive versus risky to mental health and guide educational institutions, mental health professionals, and policymakers.
